Softener, filter or reverse osmosis: which do you need?

These three get lumped together, but each one solves a different problem. Start with the problem you have.

November 6, 2026

People often call and ask for “a water filter” when what’s bothering them is scale, or ask for a softener because the water tastes bad. Neither would get the result they want, so it helps to know what each one does.

A softener stops scale

It swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium. Your soap lathers, the crust stops forming and your water heater stays cleaner. It does very little for taste.

A carbon filter fixes taste and smell

Carbon grabs chlorine and many of the compounds that make water smell or taste off. Installed at the main line, it treats the whole house. It doesn’t touch hardness.

Reverse osmosis cleans your drinking water

It forces water through a very fine membrane, which removes most dissolved material. That includes things the other two leave behind. It’s normally fitted at one kitchen tap because it’s slow and sends some water down the drain as it works.

So which one?

  • Scale and soap film: softener
  • Chlorine smell in the shower: carbon filter
  • Both of those: a softener and carbon combo
  • You want the best water you can get for drinking and cooking: reverse osmosis at the sink

Plenty of homes end up with a softener and carbon combo for the house and reverse osmosis in the kitchen. Plenty of others only need one of them. A test will tell you which group you’re in.
