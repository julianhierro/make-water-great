On a well? Test these things before you buy anything

Nobody tests a private well for you. Here’s what to check, how often, and why it matters before you pick a system.

November 6, 2026

City water suppliers have to test and treat water to meet federal standards. The EPA doesn’t regulate private wells, so that job belongs to the homeowner. The CDC recommends testing a well at least once a year.

Start with a certified lab

Have a state-certified lab check for total coliform bacteria, nitrates, pH and total dissolved solids. Your state or county health department can usually point you to one. Depending on your area, it may also suggest testing for arsenic, lead or other local concerns.

Then test for what causes the daily problems

Iron, manganese, hardness and hydrogen sulfide are what cause the stains, the metal taste and the rotten egg smell. Those can usually be measured on site, and they decide what kind of treatment system you need.

Why the order matters

A softener can’t fix a bacteria problem, and a UV lamp won’t do anything for iron. It’s also easy to buy equipment for the problem you can see and miss one you can’t. Get the results first, then size the system for the water you’ve really got.

Retest after any flood, any work on the well, or any sudden change in taste, color or smell.
