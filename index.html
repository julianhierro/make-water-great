Why this matters

Four signs your water could be better

People rarely call us after reading a water report. They call because something at home has been annoying them for months.

White crust around the faucets

Every drop of hard water that dries leaves a little calcium and magnesium behind. Give it a few months and you can see it on the showerhead, the glass door and the kettle.

Soap that never quite lathers

Those same minerals react with soap and form the sticky film you feel on your skin after a shower. You end up using more shampoo and detergent to get less done.

Appliances that give up early

Scale coats the inside of water heaters, dishwashers and pipes. A coated heating element has to work harder to warm the same tank of water.

Water you would rather not drink

A chlorine smell usually means city treatment. Rotten egg or a metal taste usually points to a well. Both have a fix, and they aren’t the same fix.

Know your number

How hard is hard?

Hardness is measured, not guessed at. Our test gives you a number, and that number decides whether you need a softener and how big it should be.

How to read your number →
Soft0 to 60 mg/Lunder 3.5 gpg
Moderately hard61 to 120 mg/L3.5 to 7 gpg
Hard121 to 180 mg/L7 to 10.5 gpg
Very hardover 180 mg/Lover 10.5 gpg

Bands from the US Geological Survey. mg/L ÷ 17.1 = grains per gallon (gpg).

Questions

What people ask us

If yours isn’t here, send us your question.

What does a water softener actually do?

It swaps the calcium and magnesium in your water for sodium or potassium using a process called ion exchange. That stops scale forming. It doesn’t remove chlorine or improve the taste much, which is why a lot of homes pair it with a carbon filter.

Do I need a softener, a filter or reverse osmosis?

They solve different problems. A softener deals with scale. A carbon filter deals with chlorine, taste and smell. Reverse osmosis cleans up the water you drink at one tap. The test tells us which of those you actually have.

What does the free water test cover?

The on-site test covers hardness, chlorine, iron, pH and total dissolved solids. It can’t tell you about bacteria, lead or PFAS. Those need a certified lab, and we’ll tell you if we think it’s worth sending a sample.

Will softened water make my drinking water salty?

Softening adds a small amount of sodium, and how much depends on how hard your water was to begin with. Most people can’t taste it. If you’re on a low-sodium diet, ask your doctor, and consider reverse osmosis at the kitchen tap or potassium instead of salt.

How much maintenance is there?

Softeners need salt topped up, usually every month or two. Carbon media and reverse osmosis filters and membranes wear out on a schedule, and we’ll give you the dates for your system when we install it.

How long does installation take?

Most installs take around half a day, depending on where your main line runs and where the equipment can go. We’ll give you a better estimate when we’ve seen the house.
